# Typography, Grids & Layout Systems

A design often contains **text, images, shapes, and empty space**.

Typography and layout systems help designers arrange these elements so that information is **clear, readable, and easy to follow**.

In the previous lesson, we explored balance, alignment, contrast, and proximity. In this lesson, we will use these principles to organize text and visual elements.

---

## 1. Typography

**Typography** is the arrangement and appearance of text in a design.

Typography is not only about choosing an attractive font. It also helps readers understand **which information is important** and **what to read first**.

### Typeface and Font

A **typeface** is a family of letter designs, such as Arial or Times New Roman.

A **font** is a particular variation of a typeface, such as Arial Bold or Arial Regular.

### Common Typeface Styles

- **Serif:** Letters have small finishing strokes. Often used in books and formal designs.
- **Sans serif:** Letters do not have these finishing strokes. Often used in interfaces, signs, and modern layouts.

## 2. Typography and Readability

Several properties affect how easily we read text:

- **Font size:** How large or small the text appears.
- **Font weight:** How thick or thin the letters appear, such as regular or bold.
- **Line spacing (leading):** The vertical distance between lines of text.
- **Letter spacing (tracking):** The space between letters across a word or line.
- **Text alignment:** How text is positioned along an edge or axis, such as left, center, or right.

A design with very small text, crowded lines, or weak contrast can be difficult to read.

> Readability is more important than decoration.

## 3. Visual Hierarchy in Text

**Visual hierarchy** helps viewers identify the most important information first.

For example, a poster might contain:

- **Title:** The largest or most noticeable text.
- **Subtitle:** Supporting information.
- **Body text:** Smaller details.

Designers can create hierarchy through **size, weight, color, spacing, and position**.

    <img src="{{ '/assets/images/lesson06/6_3.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Poster text with a clear title, subtitle and body hierarchy">

If every line of text looks equally important, viewers may not know where to begin.

## 4. Grids

A **grid** is a system of guides used to organize elements within a design.

A grid can help designers maintain:

- consistent alignment,
- regular spacing,
- clear grouping,
- and a balanced layout.

A simple grid may contain **columns**, **rows**, and **margins**.

- **Columns:** Vertical areas used to position content.
- **Rows:** Horizontal areas used to position content.
- **Margins:** Empty space between content and the edges of the page.
- **Gutters:** Spaces between columns or rows.

---

## 5. Layout Systems

A **layout** is the arrangement of text and visual elements on a page or screen.

Different layouts can communicate different ideas.

### Single-Column Layout

Content is arranged in one main vertical column. This is common in simple articles and mobile screens.

### Multi-Column Layout

Content is divided into two or more columns. This can help organize several pieces of information, as in magazines or websites.

## Summary

Typography controls how text **looks and reads**.

**Visual hierarchy** shows what information is most important.

**Grids** provide guides for alignment and spacing.

**Layout systems** organize text and visual elements into a clear composition.

These ideas work together. A good design is not simply attractive; it also makes information easy to understand.

# In-Class Practice — Typography & Layout

Open **Inkscape** and create a new document.

Design a simple **event poster** using text and basic vector shapes. You may choose an event such as a game night, art exhibition, or student workshop.

Your poster should include:

1. A **title**, **subtitle**, and **small detail text** (such as date or location).
2. At least **two different font sizes** to create hierarchy.
3. A simple **two-column grid** or clearly aligned single-column layout.
4. Consistent **margins and spacing**.
5. At least one vector shape and enough **negative space** to keep the design readable.

### Suggested Inkscape Steps

1. Use the **Text Tool (T)** to add the title and supporting text.
2. Change the font, size, and weight using the text controls.
3. Turn on a grid using **View → Page Grid**, or draw temporary guide rectangles to plan the layout.
4. Use **Object → Align and Distribute** to align your elements.
5. Adjust spacing and compare the result with and without the grid.
6. Save your design as an **SVG** and raster format file.

> The goal is not to add many decorations. The goal is to make information easy to read through intentional typography and layout.
