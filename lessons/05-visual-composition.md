# Principles of Visual Composition

Visual composition is the **arrangement of visual elements within a space**.

A composition may contain shapes, lines, colors, images, or text.  
Good composition organizes these elements so that the design is **clear, balanced, and visually meaningful**.

There is no single correct composition. However, designers use several principles to control how viewers perceive and navigate a design.

---

## 1. Balance

**Balance** refers to how visual weight is distributed within a composition.

Visual weight can be influenced by an element's:

- size,
- color,
- position,
- shape,
- and contrast.

### Symmetrical Balance

Elements are arranged similarly around a central axis.

It often creates a feeling of **stability, order, and formality**.

### Asymmetrical Balance

Different elements are arranged so that their visual weights still feel balanced.

It can create a more **dynamic and interesting** composition.

## 2. Alignment

**Alignment** creates a visual connection between elements by arranging them along common edges, centers, or axes.

Good alignment makes a composition appear organized and intentional.

<figure>
    <img src="{{ '/assets/images/lesson05/5_2.png' | relative_url }}">
    <figcaption>
        Randomly positioned objects vs aligned objects
    </figcaption>
</figure>


For example, interface buttons can share the same left edge, or several objects can be aligned around a common center.

Even when elements are separated by space, alignment can make them appear visually connected.

---

## 3. Contrast

**Contrast** is created when visual elements are noticeably different.

Examples include:

- large vs small,
- light vs dark,
- thick vs thin,
- geometric vs organic,
- saturated vs muted colors.

<figure>
    <img src="{{ '/assets/images/lesson05/5_3.png' | relative_url }}">
    <figcaption>
        Examples of different types of contrast
    </figcaption>
</figure>


Contrast can improve readability and create emphasis.

However, if everything strongly contrasts with everything else, the composition may lose hierarchy.

---

## 4. Proximity & Space

**Proximity** describes how the distance between elements influences their perceived relationship.

Objects positioned close together are usually interpreted as belonging to the same group.

Objects separated by larger spaces appear less related.


<figure>
    <img src="{{ '/assets/images/lesson05/5_4.png' | relative_url }}">
    <figcaption>
        Groups by space
    </figcaption>
</figure>


**Negative Space** The empty area around and between elements is called **negative space**.

Negative space is an active part of a composition. It can:

- separate information,
- improve readability,
- create emphasis,
- and prevent a design from feeling crowded.
---

## Summary 

The principles of composition do not work independently.

A designer might use:

**alignment** to organize elements,  
**proximity** to create groups,  
**contrast** to emphasize an important object,  
and **balance** to make the entire composition feel stable.

The goal is not simply to apply every principle. The goal is to use them intentionally to communicate clearly.

# In-Class Practice — Visual Composition

Open **Inkscape** and create a new document.

Create a simple abstract composition using basic vector shapes.

Your design should demonstrate at least **four** of the following:

- Balance
- Alignment
- Visual hierarchy
- Contrast
- Repetition / rhythm
- Proximity / negative space

Keep the composition simple. The goal is to demonstrate **intentional arrangement**, not complexity.
